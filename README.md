## what I actually do

Most of my career has been on the operations side of support. Ticket queues, people who are frustrated, machines that should have been imaged better, the same five issues showing up every Monday. I got good at that work by not treating it like a call center script.

- **troubleshooting** until it's actually root cause, not "reboot and hope"
- **asset management** so we know what we own, who's using it, and what it's supposed to look like
- **scripting / automation** for the boring loops nobody should still be clicking through
- **training** other people on those scripts so the knowledge isn't trapped in my head
- **watching trends** in tickets and turning that into time back — better docs, better images, fewer "unique" snowflakes

I progressed at every stop by making the next person's job easier, not by collecting buzzwords. If I can teach someone else to run the automation, that's the win. I like showing people how the sausage gets made.

## this page, and Cursor

The public thing attached to me right now is [Metro Byte Gaming / OpenClipper](https://metrobytegaming.com) — a local AI clipper for GTA VI and Gaming creators. Free tier, paid boosting, Google login, Docker, a GPU worker, Cloudflare in front of a machine in my world, staff tools, the whole messy product.

I didn't write it like a tutorial. I sat in Cursor and built it the way I work tickets: one real problem at a time.

Storage looking like 98GB when I'd just hung a 1TB drive? We traced it to the container still writing media on the OS disk. Splash clips going black after the move? They were living in `output/` instead of with the frontend. Staff table so raw you had to horizontally scroll to find messages? We turned it into an inbox.

Cursor's the pairing partner on that. I'm the one saying "don't overcomplicate this," "that's duplicating," "make it fit," and then checking it in the browser like a user would. The model writes, I steer, we ship, we break something else, we fix that too. That's closer to how I think support ops should feel in 2026 than a knowledge-base article nobody reads.

If you're looking at this because you care how people actually *use* Cursor: that's me. I live in it. I also spend my days helping humans who are not developers get unstuck, which is the other half of the product.
